# Glossary

Plain definitions, in the order a beginner meets them.

**Clip Studio Paint** — the drawing program, often shortened to **CSP**. Made by CELSYS.

**Companion Mode** — the Clip Studio Paint feature that lets a phone act as a colour picker. You reach it with **File ▸ Connect to smartphone**. It shows a **QR code** — a square barcode — that Iroiroppa reads from your screen.

**Pairing** — the handshake that lets Iroiroppa connect without a QR code next time. You pair once; it is remembered until you **Forget pairing**.

**Palette** — the floating window you pick colours in. You can have more than one.

**Module** — one panel inside a palette, doing one job: a set of maps, a wheel, the connection status, the history list. Modules can be moved, resized, hidden, duplicated and **popped out** into their own window.

**Float** — a module that has been popped out into its own small window.

**Auto-hide** — Iroiroppa shows itself while Clip Studio Paint is in front and hides when you switch to another program. It is on by default, and it is the reason the palette seems to vanish.

**Live sync** — updating the colour in Clip Studio Paint continuously while you drag, instead of once on release.

**Colour model** — a way of describing a colour with named parts. Iroiroppa mostly uses **OKLCH**, and can show others.

**OKLCH** — a colour model with three parts: **L** (lightness, black to white), **C** (chroma, grey to vivid) and **H** (hue, which colour it is). It is valued for matching how people perceive lightness — a "light red" really looks light.

**OKLab** — the same things as OKLCH, described as three axes rather than a circle. Iroiroppa stores colours this way internally, which keeps a colour stable when you switch models.

**Chroma** — how strong or vivid a colour is. Zero chroma is grey.

**Hue** — which colour it is: red, orange, yellow, green and so on, as an angle on a circle.

**Lightness** — how dark or light a colour is, from black to white.

**Gamut** — the range of colours a device or a document can actually hold. A wide-gamut document holds more colours than an ordinary monitor can display, and more than an sRGB file can.

**Colour profile** — a file describing what a set of colour numbers means. **ICC profile** is the standard format.

**sRGB** — the ordinary, safe colour space of the web and most screens. If you do not know your document's profile, it is almost certainly this.

**Adobe RGB** — a wider colour space used in print and photography. The same numbers mean more saturated colours than in sRGB.

**CMYK** — the print colour model: cyan, magenta, yellow, black. Print cannot show some bright screen colours, which is why print work needs **proofing**.

**Proofing** — previewing how a colour will look in another profile (often a printer's) without changing your document.

**Hex code** — a colour written as `#RRGGBB`, six digits describing red, green and blue. Always sRGB, unless something says otherwise.

**Transparent colour** — Clip Studio Paint's "no colour" slot, used by the eraser-like tools.

**Tray** — the row of small icons near the clock, bottom-right of Windows. Iroiroppa lives there while it runs; it may be hidden under the **^** arrow.
