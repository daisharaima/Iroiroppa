# The smaller panels

Four modules carry information rather than pick colour.

## Session

The top row of the default palette, and the one you will look at when something is off. It shows:

- **The connection state** — Connected, Scanning…, Connecting…, Reconnecting…, Pairing failed, Disconnected. Click the row to connect or retry.
- **The document's colour profile** — click it to choose how your document's colours should be interpreted. This is the setting behind most "the colours look wrong" surprises; see [Colour profiles and gamut](Colour-profiles-and-gamut).
- **Lock layout** — freezes the arrangement so nothing moves by accident.
- **Settings…** — opens the Settings window.
- **Disconnect** — drops the connection without forgetting the pairing.

The small note on that row says it best: the connection is automatic, and these are the only actions that need a hand.

## CSP Color

Clip Studio Paint's three colour slots, live:

- **Foreground** — the main colour you paint with.
- **Background** — the secondary colour.
- **Transparent** — the "no colour" slot.

Click a slot to make it the one your picks are written to. The slot showing the current brush colour is the one being edited.

## Color Stat

The numbers of the colour in hand: a swatch, the **hex** code, and the channel values in the current colour model.

- Click the **left half** of the hex code to copy it.
- Click the **right half** to paste a hex code in.
- **Double-click** it to type one.

That paste is the quickest way to bring an exact colour from anywhere else — a client's brand colour, a palette you were given, a web page.

## Color History

A list of colours you have picked recently, newest first. Click a row to use that colour again, and carry on from there.

It is a plain local list — it lives in your settings, it is not uploaded anywhere, and it is not shared with Clip Studio Paint's own palettes.

## Where they come from

Every panel can be shown, hidden, duplicated or popped out into its own window. If one is missing, it is in **Settings ▸ Modules** — see [Arrange your palette](Arrange-your-palette).
